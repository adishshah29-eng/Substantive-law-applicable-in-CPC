The appellant sought to amend its written statement in a suit concerning
a development agreement, and the amendment was resisted on the ground
that it would introduce an entirely new case inconsistent with the
original pleadings.

The Supreme Court undertook a comprehensive review of the law on
amendment of pleadings under Order VI Rule 17 of the Code of Civil
Procedure, 1908, and laid down the basic principles to be considered
while deciding an application for amendment: (i) whether the amendment is
necessary for determination of the real question in controversy; (ii)
whether the amendment merely elaborates the existing case or introduces a
new and inconsistent cause of action, effectively substituting one cause
of action for another or changing the fundamental nature of the suit; (iii)
whether the amendment causes prejudice to the other side that cannot be
compensated by costs; (iv) whether refusing the amendment would result in
a multiplicity of proceedings; and (v) whether the application is made
mala fide, or the delay in seeking the amendment is inordinate and
unexplained. The Court held that all amendments cannot be allowed as a
matter of course: the discretion conferred on the court by Order VI Rule
17 is circumscribed by these tests, and the proposed amendment should not
alter or substitute the cause of action originally pleaded, nor change
the fundamental nature and character of the suit or defence.

Revajeetu Builders is the leading modern authority collecting and
systematising the factors a court must weigh in deciding whether to
permit an amendment of pleadings under Order VI Rule 17 CPC.
