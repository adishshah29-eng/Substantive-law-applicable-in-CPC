The petitioner, having earlier filed and lost a writ petition under
Article 226 of the Constitution challenging an executive order on
certain grounds, later instituted a civil suit raising substantially the
same questions against the same parties. The question was whether the
dismissal of the writ petition barred the subsequent civil suit by res
judicata.

The Supreme Court held that the general principle of res judicata,
embodied in Section 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 but not
exhausted by it, is a rule of public policy founded on the need for
finality in litigation and applies as between a former writ petition
decided on merits under Article 226 and a later regular suit raising the
same matter in issue between the same parties, notwithstanding that
Section 11 in terms refers only to a former "suit." The Court held that
if the earlier writ petition was disposed of on merits -- as opposed to
being dismissed on a technical ground, such as availability of an
alternative remedy, without going into the merits -- the decision would
operate as res judicata (or on the general principle analogous to it) in
the later suit.

Gulabchand Chhotalal Parikh is a leading authority extending the
substance of the rule of res judicata to bar a subsequent civil suit
where the same matter was already decided on merits in an earlier writ
petition between the same parties, even though Section 11 CPC does not,
by its literal terms, refer to writ proceedings.
