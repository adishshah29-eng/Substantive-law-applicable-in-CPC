The question before the Supreme Court was whether a finding recorded by a
court of limited pecuniary jurisdiction on an issue directly and
substantially in issue between the parties would operate as res judicata
in a later suit between the same parties concerning a claim of higher
value, triable only by a court of unlimited jurisdiction. High Courts had
taken conflicting views on the interpretation of Explanation VIII to
Section 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (inserted by the Code of
Civil Procedure (Amendment) Act, 1976), which provides that a court of
limited jurisdiction competent to decide a matter shall be deemed a court
of competent jurisdiction for the purposes of Section 11, notwithstanding
that it was not competent to try the later suit.

The Supreme Court held that Explanation VIII was inserted precisely to
remove the earlier anomaly whereby a decision of a court of limited
pecuniary jurisdiction on an issue was held not to bind the parties in a
later suit triable by a court of unlimited jurisdiction, permitting
wasteful re-litigation of the same issue. The Court held that once an
issue has been directly and substantially in issue in a former suit
between the same parties, and has been heard and finally decided by a
court competent to decide that suit -- even if that court's general
pecuniary jurisdiction is limited -- Explanation VIII operates to make
that finding binding as res judicata in the later suit, regardless of
the forum's limited jurisdiction, so long as the issue itself was validly
decided.

Sulochana Amma is the leading authority resolving the conflict on
Explanation VIII to Section 11 CPC, holding that findings of courts of
limited pecuniary jurisdiction operate as res judicata in later suits of
higher valuation, so as to give true effect to the finality intended by
the doctrine.
