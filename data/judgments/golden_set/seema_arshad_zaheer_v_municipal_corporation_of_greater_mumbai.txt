The appellants, occupants of structures on municipal land, sought a
temporary injunction restraining the Municipal Corporation from
demolishing their structures, pending disposal of their suit challenging
the demolition notices. The trial court declined to grant an injunction;
the question before the Supreme Court was whether that refusal was
proper.

The Supreme Court reiterated that the grant of a temporary injunction
under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure requires
the applicant to satisfy the court on three elements -- the existence of
a prima facie case, that the balance of convenience is in the
applicant's favour, and that the applicant would suffer irreparable
injury if the injunction is refused -- and that these are well-settled
requirements, not merely factors to be weighed loosely. Applying the
test, the Court held that persons in unauthorised occupation of public
land cannot ordinarily establish a prima facie case for an injunction
restraining a public authority from enforcing eviction in accordance
with law, and that the balance of convenience in such cases favours the
public authority's right to reclaim and use public land for public
purposes.

The judgment is frequently cited, alongside Dalpat Kumar v Prahlad Singh,
for the settled three-fold test governing temporary injunctions, and
specifically for its application in cases concerning unauthorised
occupation of public land.
