The defendant failed to file a written statement within the time
permitted by the trial court under Order VIII Rule 1 of the Code of
Civil Procedure, 1908. The trial court, invoking Order VIII Rule 10,
proceeded to pronounce judgment against the defendant without further
opportunity, treating the consequence as automatic once the time expired.

The Supreme Court held that Order VIII Rule 10, which provides that
where a party fails to present a written statement within the time
permitted "the Court shall pronounce judgment against him, or make such
order in relation to the suit as it thinks fit," confers a discretion on
the court as to the appropriate course of action, and does not make
pronouncement of judgment against the defaulting defendant automatic or
inevitable merely upon expiry of the time fixed. The word "shall" in
Rule 10 must be read together with the alternative "or make such other
order in relation to the suit as it thinks fit," which shows that the
court retains discretion to grant further time or pass any other
appropriate order, having regard to the facts and circumstances,
rather than being compelled in every case to straightaway decree the
suit against a defendant who has defaulted in filing a written
statement.

Mohd. Yusuf v Faij Mohammad is cited for the proposition that Order VIII
Rule 10 CPC confers a discretion on the court as to the consequence of a
defendant's failure to file a written statement in time, and does not
mandate an automatic decree against the defaulting defendant.
