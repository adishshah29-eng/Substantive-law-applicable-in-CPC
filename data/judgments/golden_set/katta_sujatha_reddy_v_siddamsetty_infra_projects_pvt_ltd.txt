This appeal required the Supreme Court to consider whether the amendments
made to Sections 10, 14 and 16 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 by the
Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018 -- which substituted the earlier
discretionary regime with a mandatory entitlement to specific
performance, subject to limited statutory exceptions -- applied to suits
for specific performance filed, or agreements executed, before the
amendment came into force on 1 October 2018.

The Supreme Court held that the 2018 amendment is prospective in
operation and does not apply retrospectively to agreements executed, or
suits instituted, prior to its commencement. Suits concerning
pre-amendment agreements continue to be governed by the unamended
Sections 10, 14, 16 and 20 of the Act, under which specific performance
remained a discretionary remedy that a court could refuse even where the
plaintiff had otherwise made out a case, having regard to the conduct of
the parties and the circumstances of the transaction. Only agreements
executed on or after 1 October 2018 attract the amended regime, under
which specific performance is, subject to Sections 11(2), 14 and 16, the
rule rather than the exception.

This case is the leading authority confirming that the fundamental shift
made by the 2018 amendment -- from discretionary to (subject to limited
exceptions) mandatory specific performance -- operates only prospectively,
so that the applicable regime turns on the date of the underlying
agreement, not the date of suit or decree.
