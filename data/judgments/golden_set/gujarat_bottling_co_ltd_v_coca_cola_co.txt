The dispute concerned a franchise agreement containing a negative
covenant restraining the franchisee from manufacturing, dealing in or
otherwise being connected with competing beverage products during the
subsistence of the agreement. On termination of the franchise, the
franchisor sought a temporary injunction to enforce the negative
covenant restraining the franchisee from associating with a competing
cola brand.

The Supreme Court reiterated the settled principles governing the grant
of temporary injunctions under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 of the Code of
Civil Procedure -- prima facie case, balance of convenience and
irreparable injury -- and additionally considered the interplay between
Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (which renders agreements in
restraint of trade void) and Section 42 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963
(which permits an injunction to enforce a negative agreement even where
the affirmative part of the contract cannot be specifically enforced).
The Court held that a negative covenant operative during the subsistence
of a contract, and reasonably necessary for the protection of the
covenantee's interests, is not void under Section 27, and that in
considering whether to grant an interim injunction to enforce such a
covenant the court must additionally weigh the public interest in free
trade and competition alongside the ordinary three-fold test.

Gujarat Bottling Co is a leading authority applying the prima
facie/balance of convenience/irreparable injury framework of Order XXXIX
in the specific context of negative covenants and injunctions under
Section 42 of the Specific Relief Act.
