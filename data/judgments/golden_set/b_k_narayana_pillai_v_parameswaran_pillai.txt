The defendant in an eviction suit sought to amend the written statement
at a relatively late stage to raise an additional plea. The amendment
was disallowed by the courts below on the view that a written statement
should be treated with the same strictness as a plaint when it comes to
belated amendments.

The Supreme Court held that the object of Order VI Rule 17 of the Code of
Civil Procedure, 1908 is to allow either party to alter or amend
pleadings in such a manner and on such terms as may be just, so that the
real questions in controversy between the parties may be determined,
and that courts should adopt a liberal approach to amendment applications
unless serious injustice or irreparable prejudice is caused to the other
side, or unless the plaintiff's valuable right (such as one accrued by
limitation) would be affected. The Court further clarified that the
same degree of strictness applicable to amendment of a plaint is not
necessarily to be applied with equal rigour to amendment of a written
statement, since a defendant is permitted a wider latitude to amend the
defence to properly meet the plaintiff's claim, and refusing to permit a
defendant to raise a legitimate defence merely on technical grounds
defeats the purpose of the provision.

B.K. Narayana Pillai is cited for the proposition that courts should be
comparatively more liberal in allowing amendment of a written statement
than of a plaint, consistent with the general policy of Order VI Rule 17
favouring determination of the real controversy between the parties.
