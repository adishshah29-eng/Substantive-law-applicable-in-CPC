This appeal concerned the scope of an appellate court's power to interfere
with an order granting or refusing a temporary injunction under Order
XXXIX of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908.

The Supreme Court held that the grant or refusal of a temporary
injunction is a matter within the discretion of the trial court, and an
appellate court will not interfere with the exercise of that discretion
merely because it would itself have exercised the discretion differently
on the same facts. Interference is warranted only where the trial court
has acted arbitrarily, capriciously, perversely, or has ignored settled
principles governing the grant of injunctions -- such as the requirement
to consider prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable
injury -- resulting in a manifest miscarriage of justice. The Court
observed that an appeal against exercise of discretion is not the same
as an appeal on merits, and the appellate court must ask whether the
discretion was exercised reasonably and in accordance with settled legal
principles, not simply whether it agrees with the conclusion reached.

Wander Ltd v Antox India is the leading authority on the limited scope of
appellate review of discretionary interim injunction orders, distinct
from -- but frequently cited alongside -- Dalpat Kumar v Prahlad Singh's
substantive three-fold test for the grant of such injunctions in the
first place.
