In service litigation concerning seniority between direct recruits and
promotees, a batch of writ petitions raised a question of seniority
principles that some affected employees, though aware of the litigation
and its potential effect on them, chose not to join. Years later, they
sought to reopen the same question in fresh proceedings.

The Supreme Court, in laying down comprehensive principles governing
seniority disputes, also addressed the doctrine of constructive res
judicata, holding that a person who had knowledge of an earlier
proceeding raising a particular question and an opportunity to be
impleaded and contest it, but who deliberately or negligently omitted to
do so, cannot ordinarily be permitted to reopen the same question in
subsequent litigation; the principle underlying Section 11 of the Code
of Civil Procedure, 1908 -- and in particular Explanation IV, under
which a matter that might and ought to have been raised in the former
proceeding, but was not, is deemed to have been directly and
substantially in issue in that proceeding -- extends by analogy to bar
such belated re-litigation, in service and writ jurisdiction matters as
well as in ordinary civil suits.

This case is cited for applying the doctrine of constructive res judicata
-- rooted in Explanation IV to Section 11 CPC -- outside the strict
confines of a civil suit, to service jurisprudence and writ litigation,
where a party who had a fair opportunity to raise a plea in earlier
proceedings will be barred from raising it later.
