The plaintiff, claiming to be a co-sharer in joint family property,
sought a temporary injunction under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC
restraining the defendants from raising construction on the disputed
land pending adjudication of title and partition in the suit.

The Supreme Court reiterated that the three well-settled conditions for
the grant of a temporary injunction -- a prima facie case, balance of
convenience in favour of the applicant, and the likelihood of
irreparable injury if the injunction is refused -- must all be
established, and additionally emphasised that in property disputes
involving construction on disputed land, courts should be circumspect
about permitting construction to proceed during the pendency of the
suit, since allowing construction to continue may itself alter the
balance of convenience and cause injury incapable of being undone once
the suit is decided. The Court held that where construction on disputed
land threatens to change the nature of the property and create a fait
accompli before the suit is decided, that is itself a strong factor
going to irreparable injury and balance of convenience in favour of
maintaining the status quo through injunction pending trial.

Anand Prasad Agarwalla is cited as authority for the application of the
Dalpat Kumar three-fold test specifically to injunctions restraining
construction on suit property pending trial, where preserving the status
quo is necessary to avoid the dispute being rendered infructuous.
