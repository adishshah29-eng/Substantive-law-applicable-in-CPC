The plaintiff sought specific performance of an agreement for sale. The
trial court and first appellate court dismissed the suit on the ground
that the plaintiff had failed to plead and prove that he was, at all
material times, ready and willing to perform his part of the contract as
required under Section 16(c) of the Specific Relief Act, 1963 (as it then
stood).

The Supreme Court held that readiness and willingness to perform the
contract is a condition precedent, mandatory in nature, for obtaining a
decree of specific performance, and that it is not sufficient for the
plaintiff to merely aver readiness and willingness in the plaint; it must
be proved by the evidence on record, having regard to the plaintiff's
conduct before and after filing the suit read as a whole. Whether the
plaintiff was ready and willing is a question to be decided on the facts
and circumstances of each case, and continuous readiness and willingness
must be shown from the date of the agreement until the date of the
hearing.

The Court set aside the concurrent findings of the courts below on the
facts of that case, holding that the record did in fact establish the
requisite readiness and willingness, and decreed specific performance.

This case is a leading authority on Section 16 of the Specific Relief
Act, establishing that a plaintiff seeking specific performance must
both plead and affirmatively prove continuous readiness and willingness
to perform his own obligations under the contract; failure to do so is
fatal to the suit.
