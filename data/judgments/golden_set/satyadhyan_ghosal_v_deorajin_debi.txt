In a suit for ejectment, an earlier interlocutory order on the question of
whether the defendant was a tenant protected under rent control
legislation had attained finality, no appeal having been filed against
it. At a later stage of the same suit, the same question was sought to
be re-agitated.

The Supreme Court held that the principle of res judicata, though
statutorily embodied in Section 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908
in terms confined to a subsequent "suit," rests on a wider foundation:
the need to give finality to judicial decisions so that parties are not
vexed twice over the same matter, and so that the machinery of the
courts is not used to permit endless re-litigation of issues already
decided between the same parties. The Court held that the general
principle of res judicata applies as between two stages of the same
litigation, so that if an order made in the course of a suit -- whether
interlocutory or otherwise -- decides a matter in controversy and that
order is not appealed from, or is upheld on appeal, that decision is
final between the parties for all subsequent stages of the same
proceeding, and cannot be reopened at a later stage of the same suit,
even though Section 11 itself, by its terms, speaks only of a former
"suit" and not of an earlier stage of the same suit.

Satyadhyan Ghosal is the leading authority for the proposition that the
underlying rule of res judicata is not exhausted by Section 11 CPC and
extends, on general principle, to bar re-agitation of matters already
finally decided at an earlier stage of the very same suit.
