The question before the Supreme Court concerned the extent of a trial
court's discretion to extend time for filing a written statement beyond
the outer limit set by the proviso to Order VIII Rule 1 of the Code of
Civil Procedure, 1908, in light of the earlier decisions in Kailash v
Nanhku and Salem Advocate Bar Association construing that limit as
directory rather than mandatory.

The Supreme Court held that although the time limit prescribed in Order
VIII Rule 1 is directory, that does not mean it may be routinely and
casually disregarded; a defendant seeking extension of time beyond the
ninety-day outer limit must place cogent material before the court
explaining the delay, and the discretion to extend time must be
exercised sparingly, only in exceptional cases, for reasons to be
recorded, keeping in view that the very purpose of the amendment
introducing the time limit was to curb the practice of unduly prolonging
suits through delayed filing of written statements. The Court held that
the general discretionary power of courts under Section 148 of the Code
to enlarge time does not override the specific object of the proviso to
Order VIII Rule 1, and that an overly liberal or routine grant of
extensions would defeat the legislative object even though the provision
is, strictly, directory in form.

R.N. Jadi & Brothers is cited alongside Kailash v Nanhku for clarifying
that, while the time limit in Order VIII Rule 1 is directory and
extendable, the discretion to extend must be exercised with restraint
and only on cogent grounds, not as a matter of course.
