Two connected suits between the same parties, raising overlapping
questions of title, were dismissed by the trial court and one was
appealed while the other was allowed to remain undisturbed and become
final. The question was whether the decision that had become final in
the suit not appealed operated as res judicata in the appeal from the
other suit.

The Supreme Court held that for a decision in a former suit to operate
as res judicata under Section 11 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908,
what matters is that the matter was directly and substantially in issue
and was heard and finally decided, not merely the label of "suit" versus
"appeal": a decision on merits that has become final, whether because no
appeal was filed against it or because an appeal was disposed of on
merits, has the same res judicata effect as a decree in a fully
contested former suit. The Court further clarified that where an appeal
is dismissed on a purely technical ground -- without a decision on
merits -- the underlying trial court decision does not thereby acquire
the same conclusive effect as a decision that was actually heard and
finally decided on merits by the appellate forum.

Sheodan Singh is cited for clarifying that the essential requirement for
res judicata under Section 11 CPC is a final decision on merits between
the same parties on the matter in issue, and that this requirement is
unaffected by whether finality was reached by a decree standing
unappealed or by an appellate decision on the merits.
