An application for amendment of the plaint was made after the
commencement of trial, and was resisted on the ground that the proviso to
Order VI Rule 17 of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 -- inserted by the
Code of Civil Procedure (Amendment) Act, 2002 -- barred any amendment
sought after commencement of trial.

The Supreme Court held that the proviso to Order VI Rule 17, which
prohibits amendment after the trial has commenced "unless the Court
comes to the conclusion that in spite of due diligence, the party could
not have raised the matter before the commencement of trial," was
inserted to curtail the practice of seeking amendments belatedly to
delay proceedings, and courts must exercise caution before allowing
amendments at that late stage. However, the discretion under the proviso
is not to be exercised so strictly as to defeat amendments that are
genuinely necessary to decide the real controversy between the parties;
where the party seeking amendment can show that, despite due diligence,
the matter could not have been raised earlier -- for instance, because
the necessity for it arose only from a subsequent development or from
material that came to light after the trial had commenced -- the
proviso's bar does not apply and the amendment may still be allowed.

South Konkan Distilleries clarifies the practical operation of the 2002
proviso to Order VI Rule 17: post-trial amendments are permissible only
on proof of due diligence and an inability to have raised the matter
earlier, marking a deliberately stricter standard than for pre-trial
amendments, which continue to be viewed liberally.
