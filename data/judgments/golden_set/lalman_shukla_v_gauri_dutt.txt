The defendant's nephew ran away from home. The defendant sent several of
his servants, including the plaintiff Lalman Shukla, to search for the
boy, and separately announced a reward of Rs. 501 to anyone who found
him, by way of handbills distributed after the plaintiff had already
left in search. The plaintiff found the boy without knowledge of the
reward offer and later sued for the reward on his return.

The Allahabad High Court held that the plaintiff's claim must fail. To
convert a proposal into a promise under the Indian Contract Act, 1872,
acceptance of the proposal must be communicated to the offeree and the
offeree must act with knowledge of the offer. Since the plaintiff was
ignorant of the reward offer at the time he found and brought back the
boy, there could be no consensus ad idem, and therefore no contract came
into existence merely because his act happened to coincide with what the
offer required. An offer of which a person has no knowledge at the time
of performance cannot be regarded as accepted by that performance.

This decision is the leading Indian authority for the rule that a valid
acceptance -- and hence a valid contract by conduct -- requires the
offeree's knowledge of the offer at the time of the act said to
constitute acceptance; a person cannot accept what he does not know
exists.
